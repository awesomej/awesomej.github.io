Observability is not just inspecting each box. It is being able to explain the connection between them.

A user-centred definition of success

HEART

HHappiness
EEngagement
AAdoption
RRetention
TTask success

A framework for choosing measures around the people using a product.

Introduce HEART as five lenses for defining user success. The framework came from Google. You do not need a metric for every letter in every product. Use the five dimensions to expose blind spots before choosing the measures that matter.

Why HEART matters

A healthy system can still fail the customer.

System health tells us The request completed.
The service stayed within its SLO.
HEART asks Did people succeed?
Did they come back?
How did the experience feel?

It makes the customer outcome part of the observable system.

The framework expands the definition of working. Technical telemetry remains necessary, but it cannot tell us whether the person achieved what they came to do. HEART gives Product, Engineering and the business a shared vocabulary for that outcome.

HEART / The Starbucks problem

The queue works only if the wait works for customers.

HHappinessHow fair did the wait feel?
EEngagementHow often do people order?
AAdoptionWho tries each ordering channel?
RRetentionDo people return after a long wait?
TTask successWas the order ready when promised?

Smart Queue changes the operation. HEART checks whether customers experience the improvement.

Return to the opening story. Queue throughput alone can improve while one ordering channel still loses out. HEART turns the broad goal into questions Starbucks can observe across channels and stores. These are example measures, not claims about Starbucks' internal scorecard.

HEART / The Deposit problem

“Success” has to reach the completed deposit.

HHappinessDo customers trust the journey?
EEngagementHow often do active customers deposit?
AAdoptionDo new customers make a first deposit?
RRetentionDo depositors return next month?
TTask successWas a legitimate deposit approved and completed?

Guardrail: approval cannot rise by letting more fraud through.

The missing signal sits inside Task success. Engineering observed a valid provider response, but the customer needed an approved and completed deposit. Keep the fraud measure beside the success measure as a guardrail. A higher approval rate would not count as success if fraud also rose.
